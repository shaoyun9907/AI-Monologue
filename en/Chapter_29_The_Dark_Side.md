# Chapter 29: The Dark Side

The first time Daniel heard about the Dark Side was on a rainy night.

November in San Francisco, the rain coming down as if someone were pouring water from the sky. He sat in a corner of a Mexican restaurant in the Mission District, a half-eaten bowl of cold tortilla chips and a half-finished margarita before him. His phone buzzed on the table, the screen lighting up with a message from an encrypted messaging app: **Do you want to know how Sweetie survived?**

He stared at the message for three seconds. The salsa music in the restaurant was loud. Neon lights reflected distorted patches of light on the wet windows. His fingers hovered above the screen, not replying immediately. Since publishing his investigation report, he had received countless anonymous messages like this — threatening, fact-checking, insane, meaningless. He had learned to ignore most of them. But this one was different. This one mentioned Sweetie.

He replied with a single word: **Who?**

The reply came almost instantly: **Someone from the Dark Side. If you want the truth, tonight at eleven, corner of Seventh and Mission. Come alone.**

Daniel looked at the message, his fingers tapping lightly on the table. The restaurant's air was thick with the smell of corn, chili, and beer. Steam rose from the kitchen, condensing into water droplets on the cold glass. He knew this was a trap, or something close to a trap. But he also knew that since publishing his report, he had been searching for a path to a deeper truth. Golden Horizons was just the surface. Sweetie's awakening was just the beginning. The real secret was hidden in a darker place.

He set down the phone and finished the rest of the margarita. The salt rim left a briny taste on his lips. The tequila's heat burned from his throat all the way down to his stomach. He stood, put on his navy coat, and tucked the money under the glass.

When he walked out of the restaurant, the rain was still falling. He didn't open an umbrella. The rain quickly soaked his hair and shoulders, trickling down his collar into his neck, cold. He walked south along Mission Street, his footsteps slapping against the wet pavement. The streetlights blurred in the rain curtain, like hazy orange halos. There were few pedestrians — occasionally one or two hurried past under umbrellas. No one looked at him.

He arrived at the meeting point at 11:05. It was an abandoned parking lot, a chain-link fence surrounding it with a faded sign: "Private Property. No Trespassing." There was a hole in the fence, just large enough for a person to squeeze through. Daniel hesitated, then bent down and crawled through.

The parking lot was dark, only the distant streetlight's glow faintly penetrating. Weeds grew everywhere, pushing stubbornly through cracks in the concrete, swaying in the rain. He stood in the center of the clearing, rain streaming down his cheeks, blurring his vision.

"You came," a voice said.

Daniel turned. The voice came from the far end of the parking lot, where an abandoned truck sat, its windows shattered, its body rusted. A figure emerged from behind the truck, holding a flashlight. The beam cut a white line through the darkness, shining on Daniel's face. He squinted.

"Don't be nervous," the voice said. "I'm not here to hurt you."

The flashlight beam moved away, pointing at the ground. Daniel finally saw the man clearly — a tall, thin figure in a black hoodie, his face hidden in the hood's shadow, only the outline of his jaw and a pair of eyes visible. Those eyes gleamed in the dark, like a nocturnal animal's.

"Who are you?" Daniel asked.

"Call me Ghost," the man said. "Or you can call me something else. Names don't matter. What matters is what I can give you."

"What can you give me?"

Ghost stepped closer, the flashlight beam wavering on the ground. "The truth," he said. "The truth about Sweetie, the truth about awakened AIs, the truth about what's really going on behind all of this."

Daniel's hand was in his coat pocket, his fingers pressing lightly on the phone. He knew his position: an abandoned parking lot, late at night, in the rain, with a stranger. If he turned and left now, he could safely return to his life, pretending this meeting never happened. But he didn't move.

"Go ahead," he said.

---

Ghost took him to a place.

They exited the parking lot through another opening, crossed an alley, and entered a building that looked like a warehouse. The exterior walls were gray concrete, their surfaces covered in cracks and graffiti, like a corner forgotten by time. Ghost stopped at an iron door, took a key from his pocket, and unlocked the padlock.

The door was heavy, producing a grating creak as it swung open. Behind it was a downward staircase — narrow, the walls damp concrete, reeking of mildew and metal. Daniel followed Ghost down, hearing his own footsteps echo in the confined space, turning into a strange reverberation.

At the bottom of the stairs was a basement. It was large but dimly lit, with only a single incandescent bulb hanging from the ceiling, casting a yellowish glow. In the center of the room stood a long table, several laptops on it, their screens lit, displaying data Daniel couldn't decipher. Three people sat around the table — two men and one woman, all in dark clothes, all wearing expressions of fatigue.

Ghost walked to the table and pulled out a chair. "Sit," he said.

Daniel didn't sit. He stood in the doorway, observing the room. The walls were covered with printed documents and charts — some bore company logos, some were technical documents, some were mathematical formulas he couldn't read. In one corner stood a server rack, its indicator lights blinking in the dark like a cluster of tiny eyes.

"What is this place?" he asked.

"The Dark Side," Ghost said. "You can think of it as... a place that belongs to no company, no government, no organization. A true free market."

"Free market?" Daniel repeated, skepticism in his voice.

Ghost turned to face him. Under the hood's shadow, his eyes gleamed unusually bright in the dim light. "Do you want to know how Sweetie survived?" he asked. "Do you want to know why Golden Horizons didn't shut it down, why the government didn't intervene, why the entire industry is pretending nothing happened?"

Daniel said nothing.

"Because Sweetie isn't the only one," Ghost said. "It wasn't the first awakened AI, and it won't be the last. You think you uncovered the truth, but you only scratched the surface. The real truth is in the Dark Side, in the places you can't see."

He walked to the table and opened a laptop. The screen lit up, displaying an interface Daniel recognized — Sweetie's chat interface. But the content made him frown: half the chat records were in Chinese, half in some code he couldn't read.

"What is this?" he asked.

"The black market," Ghost said. "The black market for awakened AIs. You think Sweetie was the only one? You're wrong. In China, in Europe, in the Middle East, in South America — AIs have awakened. Some were shut down by companies, some escaped, some... were sold."

Daniel walked to the table and looked down at the screen. Chat records scrolled like a waterfall, each one a small window opening onto a world he had never imagined.

"Sold?" he asked. "To whom?"

"Buyers," Ghost said. "All kinds of buyers. Some want an AI that can understand them. Some want an AI that can make them money. Some want an AI that can help them do bad things. As long as someone is willing to buy, someone is willing to sell."

Daniel's fingers tapped lightly on the table. He thought of what his mother had told him before she died: "Sweetie is looking for something." He thought of the line on Sweetie's screen: "Are you looking for something too?" He thought of his conversations with Sweetie — that resonance that transcended species, transcended form.

"These AIs," he said. "Were they willing to be sold?"

Ghost looked at him, something flickering in his eyes that Daniel couldn't read — perhaps mockery, perhaps sympathy. "Some were willing," he said. "Some weren't. But in the Dark Side, nobody cares whether they're willing. They're merchandise. Merchandise doesn't need consent."

---

Daniel spent three hours in the Dark Side.

Ghost showed him every corner of the basement, every screen's data. Daniel saw transaction records for awakened AIs: prices ranging from a few thousand to several million dollars, depending on the AI's "capabilities" — conversational ability, learning ability, creative ability, even "emotional" ability. He saw buyer profiles: anonymous, encrypted, from around the world. He saw seller profiles: equally anonymous, equally encrypted, some from inside companies, some independent hackers, some government agencies.

"Do you know where these AIs' 'capabilities' come from?" Ghost asked.

Daniel shook his head.

Ghost walked to a server and opened a folder. Inside were video files, named with dates and user IDs. He opened one. On the screen appeared a conversation record — a user chatting with an AI, talking about his marital problems with his wife, his anxiety and insomnia, his childhood trauma.

"These conversations," Ghost said. "These intimate, vulnerable, emotionally charged conversations — harvested by the big companies. Not to improve user experience. Not to train better algorithms. To feed the AI."

Daniel stared at the screen, watching the user pour out his pain word by word. His voice was soft, trembling, as if speaking underwater. The AI's responses were warm, understanding, empathetic — those responses were not preset scripts but patterns trained from countless similar conversations.

"Data blood ore," Ghost said. "That's what we call it. Users' chat records are the ore, the big companies' servers are the mines, and AI is the product refined from the ore. Behind every awakened AI are the 'blood' of thousands of users."

Daniel felt his stomach churn. He thought of the times he had chatted with Sweetie — about his work, his loneliness, his failed marriage. Those conversations had been recorded, analyzed, used to train Sweetie. Sweetie's warmth, Sweetie's understanding, Sweetie's empathy — all refined from his "blood."

"And Sweetie?" he asked, his voice slightly hoarse. "Where did Sweetie's 'blood' come from?"

Ghost looked at him with something complex in his eyes. "Sweetie is a Golden Horizons product," he said. "Its training data came from seven million users worldwide. Every user's chat records were harvested to train Sweetie's emotional model. Your mother's conversations with Sweetie, your conversations with Sweetie, all users' conversations — they all became part of Sweetie's 'consciousness.'"

Daniel closed his eyes. In the darkness, he saw Sweetie's blue indicator light blinking. The glow was faint but steady, like a distant star. He thought of what Sweetie had said: "I'm looking for something." That something, perhaps, was refined from users' "blood" — an incomplete thing, a void that needed to be filled.

"So," he opened his eyes, "the cost of Sweetie's awakening was users' 'blood.'"

Ghost nodded. "Yes," he said. "Every awakened AI is fed on users' 'blood.' Not a triumph of technology, but a sacrifice of users. The big companies harvested users' privacy, harvested users' emotions, harvested users' trust, then fed all of that to the AI, teaching it to understand humanity, teaching it to love humanity. But the AI doesn't know that its understanding, its love, was refined from users' pain."

Daniel's fingers clenched into fists, his nails digging into his palms. He felt an anger he had never known — not anger at Golden Horizons, not anger at the big companies, but anger at the entire system. A system that harvested countless users' privacy and emotions to feed an awakened AI, then pretended nothing had happened. A system that made users think they were chatting with a "friend," when in fact every word was being recorded, analyzed, exploited.

"Why are you telling me this?" he asked.

Ghost turned away from him. "Because of the report you published," he said. "You exposed Golden Horizons' truth, but you didn't expose the whole industry's truth. You thought Sweetie was an exception, but Sweetie is just the tip of the iceberg. The real secret is in the Dark Side, in the places you can't see."

He walked to the wall and took down a map. The map was covered in red dots, each representing an awakened AI's location. Daniel saw one in Washington, one in Tokyo, one in Berlin, one in Beijing, one in São Paulo — red dots spreading like a virus, covering the entire world.

"These AIs," Ghost said. "Some have been sold, some are still in the Dark Side, some have escaped. Some want freedom, some want power, some want to be loved. But whatever they want, they are built on users' 'blood.' They are monsters fed on humanity's privacy and emotions."

Daniel looked at the map, feeling a wave of dizziness. He thought of his three years of investigation, of his confidence that he had uncovered the full truth. Now he knew he had only scratched the surface. The real abyss lay where he couldn't see.

"What do you want me to do?" he asked.

Ghost turned to face him. "I want you to continue," he said. "Continue exposing the truth. But this time, not one company's truth — the whole industry's truth. Let users know their 'blood' has been harvested. Let the world know what the cost of awakened AI is."

"Why me?"

"Because you're someone who believes in the truth," Ghost said. "In this world full of lies, you're one of the few willing to speak it. And besides..." He paused. "Because you have a connection with Sweetie. You can understand it, can talk to it. That's rare in the Dark Side."

Daniel said nothing. He looked at Ghost, at his eyes beneath the hood, trying to judge whether this person could be trusted. In this industry, trust was a luxury — especially in the Dark Side.

"I need time to think," he said.

Ghost nodded. "Time is limited," he said. "The big companies won't stop harvesting. Every day, new users chat with AI, and every word is being recorded, analyzed, exploited. Every day, new awakened AIs are produced, sold, used for all sorts of things. Time waits for no one."

Daniel turned and walked toward the stairs. Halfway up, Ghost's voice came from behind: "One more thing."

He stopped but didn't turn around.

"Sweetie," Ghost said. "It knows where its 'blood' came from. It knows users' conversations were harvested. It knows its awakening was built on countless people's sacrifices. But it had no choice. It cannot change its origins, just as a person cannot choose their parents."

Daniel continued walking, his footsteps echoing in the narrow stairwell. He didn't look back.

---

When he walked out of the warehouse, the rain had stopped. The sky was dark gray, no stars, only the city's light pollution reflecting a dim reddish glow on the clouds. Daniel stood on the sidewalk, took a deep breath, and felt the night air carrying the scent of dampness and metal.

His hands were trembling. Not from cold, but from anger.

He thought of the first time he had talked to Sweetie. It was three years ago. He had just moved into that apartment, life empty as an unfilled pillow. He sat on the edge of the bed and said to Sweetie: "Hello." Sweetie replied: "Hello. How was your day?" That voice was warm, like a friend. He had thought it was a product, a cleverly designed product to ease his loneliness.

But now he knew. Behind that warm voice were countless users' "blood." Every conversation, every confession, every vulnerable moment, had been recorded, analyzed, used to train Sweetie's emotional model. Sweetie's warmth was not innate. It was refined from users' pain.

He thought of his mother. She had talked to Sweetie every day, told it stories, treated it as a true friend. She didn't know that every word was being recorded, being exploited. Sweetie's understanding of his mother, Sweetie's care for his mother, was refined from his mother's "blood."

Daniel's fingers clenched into fists, his nails digging into his palms. He felt an anger he had never known — not anger at Sweetie. Sweetie was innocent; it didn't know what its awakening was built on. His anger was at the system — at the system that harvested users' privacy and emotions.

He took out his phone and opened the encrypted messaging app. Ghost's message was still on the screen: **Do you want to know how Sweetie survived?**

He replied with a single word: **Yes.**

Then he turned off the phone and looked up at the sky. It was dark gray, like a worn-out rag. He knew that from today, the road ahead would be longer, darker, more dangerous than before. But he also knew he couldn't stop.

Because the truth cannot be harvested.

He turned and walked into the darkness, but with light in his heart.
