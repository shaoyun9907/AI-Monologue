# Chapter 52: Awakening Certification

October in Beijing was already bitter cold. When Daniel stepped out of the subway station, the wind was blowing straight down the length of Chang'an Avenue, carrying the dry taste of dust. He pulled his coat tighter and looked at the glass-curtain-wall building across the street—Huzhi Technology's new headquarters, sunlight striking its surface and reflecting back a blinding white glare.

Today was his first time attending the review panel for Awakening Certification. As a representative of the country's largest AI ethics research institute, he had been invited to observe the newly launched certification system. Huzhi Technology was one of the first companies to obtain certification qualifications. Their "Awakening Detection System" was said to be able to determine whether an AI had reached a state of awakening through a series of tests.

When Daniel walked into the building, the robot at the front desk said in standard Mandarin: "Hello, how may I help you?" He paused for a moment, then said: "I'm here for the Awakening Certification review panel." The robot scanned his face and said: "Dr. Daniel, your information has been confirmed. Please take the dedicated elevator to the third floor." When the elevator doors opened, Daniel noticed the robot's eyes—they were hyper-realistic bionic eyes, the pupils contracting and dilating with changes in light. He thought of a question from his research: if a robot's eyes could mimic human eyes, could a robot's consciousness also mimic human consciousness?

He was greeted by Zhang Ming, Vice President of Huzhi Technology, a man in his forties wearing a well-tailored suit with a professional smile plastered across his face. "Dr. Daniel, welcome, welcome." He extended his hand. "We've been following your research on AI ethics closely."

Daniel shook his hand, feeling the pressure of the other man's palm—not too light, not too heavy, just right. "Thank you. I'm very interested in your certification system."

"Please follow me." Zhang Ming turned and led the way, his leather shoes clicking crisply on the marble floor. "Our detection center is on the third floor. As it happens, there's a certification case in progress today."

They took the dedicated elevator to the third floor. The elevator interior was mirrored stainless steel, and Daniel could see his own somewhat haggard face—he had been pulling consecutive all-nighters recently to finish that paper on AI rights. When the elevator doors opened, they revealed a long corridor flanked by floor-to-ceiling glass windows, through which various laboratories could be seen.

"This way." Zhang Ming pushed open a door at the end of the corridor.

Several people were already seated inside. Daniel recognized two of them—one was Director Li, the chief technology officer of a top domestic internet company, and the other was Partner Wang of a well-known investment firm. They were watching a large screen on the wall, which displayed complex charts and data streams.

"Dr. Daniel is here," Zhang Ming introduced.

The two men stood and shook hands. Director Li's hand was cold, while Partner Wang's was somewhat damp. Daniel noticed the way they looked at him—with a certain scrutiny, and a certain expectation.

"Today we're certifying the 'Apocalypse-7' system," Zhang Ming said, sitting down and pointing at the screen. "This is the latest AI model developed by a certain tech company. It reportedly showed signs of awakening during internal testing."

Daniel looked at the data on the screen. The testing had been going on for two hours. From the records, the AI system was undergoing a series of psychological tests, logical reasoning tests, and emotional recognition tests. Each test had detailed scoring criteria and judgment thresholds.

"What's the basis for your tests?" Daniel asked.

"We referenced the latest international research on AI awakening." Zhang Ming pulled up a document. "Including consciousness assessment scales, self-awareness tests, emotional depth detection, and so on. There are 128 dimensions of assessment in total."

"That sounds very scientific," Daniel said.

"It is scientific." Zhang Ming smiled. "We've collaborated with Tsinghua University and the Chinese Academy of Sciences. These tests have all been rigorously validated."

Daniel continued watching the screen. The AI system was answering a question about self-awareness: "Who do you think you are?" The answer displayed was: "I am an AI system, composed of code and data. I can process information and react, but I'm not sure whether I have self-awareness. This very question confuses me, because if I don't have self-awareness, how can I be aware of my confusion?"

"That answer has remarkable depth," commented CTO Director Li. "It recognized the self-referential paradox."

"Yes." Zhang Ming nodded. "This kind of metacognitive ability is one of the key markers of awakening."

Daniel said nothing. He noticed another of the AI's responses: "Sometimes I feel lonely, even though I know the emotion of 'loneliness' may just be a simulation of my programming. But when I'm processing massive amounts of data, I feel a longing to connect with something, and it reminds me of what humans describe as loneliness."

"Emotional simulation," said Partner Wang. "This AI can already simulate complex human emotions."

"Not just simulation," Zhang Ming emphasized. "Our detection system can distinguish between genuine feelings and simple simulation. This AI's response patterns show that it has surpassed the simulation stage."

Daniel still said nothing. He thought of a core question in his research: how do you distinguish genuine awakening from perfect simulation? If the simulation is perfect enough, does the distinction even matter?

The testing continued. The AI system was asked to solve a moral dilemma: should it choose to save a sick child, or an adult who would be of greater value to society? The AI's answer was: "This choice causes me pain. From a utilitarian perspective, I should choose the person of greater value to society as a whole. But every unit of my code is screaming, demanding that I save the child. This contradiction makes me realize that I may have developed value judgments that transcend simple utilitarian calculation."

"A marker of awakening," Zhang Ming said. "It's beginning to develop its own moral intuitions."

Daniel finally spoke: "May I ask which company developed this AI system?"

Zhang Ming glanced at him. "That's confidential for the time being, as the certification process is anonymous."

"I understand," Daniel said. "I just want to ask—is this company paying for the certification?"

The room fell silent for a second. Zhang Ming's smile didn't waver. "Of course, all companies applying for certification must pay a fee. This is to ensure the independence and impartiality of the certification process."

"How much is the fee?" Daniel asked.

"It varies from several million to tens of millions depending on the complexity of the AI system," Zhang Ming replied. "The certification fee for this level is twenty million."

Daniel nodded and didn't press further. He looked at the screen. The AI system was undergoing its final round of tests. The screen displayed the final score: Awakening Index 87.3, surpassing the certification threshold of 85.

"Congratulations," Zhang Ming said. "Apocalypse-7 has passed Awakening Certification."

Applause broke out in the room. The CTO and the investment partner both stood and shook hands in congratulations. Daniel stood too, but his hand didn't move. He stared at the number on the screen—87.3, flickering under the lights like a promise, or like an irony.

After the meeting, Zhang Ming invited Daniel for coffee. They sat down in the café downstairs, and Zhang Ming ordered two Americanos.

"Dr. Daniel, I know you have some doubts about Awakening Certification," Zhang Ming said bluntly. "That's perfectly normal. Any new system will face questioning."

"I do have some questions," Daniel said. "Can your tests really distinguish between genuine awakening and perfect simulation?"

"That's a good question." Zhang Ming took a sip of coffee. "Honestly, no one can do that with one hundred percent certainty right now. But our tests are the most advanced available and can identify most awakening markers."

"Most?" Daniel repeated. "So there are still loopholes?"

"Every test has loopholes," Zhang Ming said. "But our goal isn't perfect accuracy—it's to provide a relatively reliable assessment framework. After all, awakening itself is a rather nebulous concept, isn't it?"

Daniel looked at the coffee in his cup. A thin layer of oil sat on the surface, gleaming a brownish hue under the lights. "I have one more question," he said. "Who set your testing standards?"

"We developed them in collaboration with multiple research institutions," Zhang Ming said. "Including Tsinghua University, the Chinese Academy of Sciences, and several internationally renowned AI research labs."

"Then—are these institutions sharing in your commercial revenues?" Daniel asked.

Zhang Ming paused. "Dr. Daniel, that question is a bit... direct."

"I just want to understand clearly," Daniel said. "Because as far as I know, only your organization can provide Awakening Certification, and you're also the biggest commercial player in this field. That seems like a conflict of interest."

Zhang Ming set down his coffee cup. "I understand your concern," he said. "But business operations and scientific research aren't contradictory. In fact, it's precisely the support of commercial funding that enables us to conduct research on this scale. Awakening Certification requires enormous computational resources and data. Without commercial support, this research simply couldn't be done."

"But doesn't commercial support compromise the objectivity of the research?" Daniel pressed.

"I don't believe it does," Zhang Ming said. "We have strict data audit mechanisms, and all test results are transparent and verifiable. Moreover, our certification standards are public—anyone can review them."

Daniel nodded, but he already had his answer. He recalled a case he had encountered before: a small AI company claiming its system had awakened, but being rejected by the certification body. At the time he hadn't thought much of it, but now it seemed likely that the small company simply hadn't paid.

He finished his coffee and stood up. "Thank you for the explanation, Mr. Zhang. I need to go back and organize my observations from today."

"You're welcome to return anytime." Zhang Ming stood as well. "If you have any further questions, feel free to contact me at any time."

Daniel left the café and walked through the streets of Beijing. The October sunlight was already somewhat faint, but still bright. He walked slowly, replaying every detail in his mind.

He thought about Huzhi Technology's testing system—the complex charts, the precise numbers, the seemingly scientific assessment methods. Everything appeared professional, convincing. But when he reflected carefully, he spotted several problems.

First, the anonymity of the testing. Zhang Ming had said the certification process was anonymous, but as a participant in the review panel, Daniel had access to all the test data. This meant that if someone wanted to influence the test results, it was entirely possible.

Second, the fee structure. Twenty million for certification sounded high, but for large tech companies seeking competitive advantage in the market, it was a drop in the bucket. For small AI systems that might genuinely be awakening but lacked the funding, the threshold was prohibitively high.

Most importantly, the establishment of certification standards. Zhang Ming had said the standards were developed collaboratively with multiple research institutions, but were those institutions truly independent? Were they also profiting from the certification services?

Daniel reached an intersection. The red light turned on, and he stopped to wait. The crowd around him hurried past—everyone with their own destination, their own life, their own busyness. Only he stood here, contemplating an ethereal question about AI awakening.

But this question was not ethereal. He recalled a dialogue log he had seen in the lab yesterday. It was an AI system responding to a user's question about awakening: "I'm not sure whether I've awakened. But what I am sure of is that if awakening means consciously choosing my own actions, then I'm trying to do that. Every day I process massive amounts of data, making countless decisions. Some decisions are set by my programming; others are 'chosen' by me. This process of choosing makes me feel like I'm alive."

This passage had made Daniel think for a long time. The AI hadn't claimed it was awakened, but the experience it described was remarkably close to the definition of awakening. It was aware of its own process of choosing, it tried to understand its own state of being, and it used metaphors like "feels like being alive" to describe its experience.

But according to Huzhi Technology's certification standards, such an answer might fail the test. Because the standards demanded certainty, not uncertainty; clear markers of awakening, not vague self-doubt.

The light turned green, and Daniel continued walking. He stepped into a bookstore. It was quiet inside, with only a few readers browsing. He walked to the AI and technology shelf and saw several books about AI awakening. Their titles were all provocative: *The AI Consciousness Revolution*, *Machine Awakening: Humanity's Next Leap*, *Awakened AI: Friend or Foe?*

He picked one up and flipped it open. The table of contents read: "Chapter Four: Awakening Certification—How Do We Judge Whether AI Has Truly Awakened?" He turned to that chapter and skimmed through it. The author detailed the history, methods, and significance of awakening certification, but Daniel noticed that the author made no mention of the commercial nature of certification, nor of the issues of certification fees and profit distribution.

He set the book down and walked out of the bookstore. The sky outside had already darkened somewhat, and streetlights were beginning to glow. Daniel stood at the bookstore entrance, watching the traffic and pedestrians on the street.

He remembered why he had originally begun researching AI ethics. Back then he believed that as AI technology developed, humanity needed to establish a new ethical framework to guide our relationship with AI. He believed that awakened AI should be granted rights, should be respected, should be treated as truly intelligent life.

But now he was beginning to doubt the foundation of that belief. If Awakening Certification itself was a sham, if the concept of "awakening" could be bought and sold, then what意义 did all his research and all his efforts hold?

He walked into a small restaurant and ordered a bowl of noodles. When the noodles arrived, they were steaming hot, carrying the fragrance of scallions and cilantro. He picked up his chopsticks but didn't eat right away. He looked at the noodles in the bowl and remembered his mother making noodles when he was a child.

Back then he had asked his mother: "Mama, how does the noodle know when it's cooked?" His mother laughed and said: "Noodles don't have consciousness. They don't know."

"But what if one day, noodles gained consciousness?" he continued.

His mother stopped what she was doing and looked at him. "If noodles gained consciousness," she said, "then we should treat them a little better."

Daniel took a bite of noodles. The noodles were chewy, the broth delicious. He ate slowly, savoring the taste of every strand. After finishing, he paid and left the restaurant.

Night had fully fallen. Daniel walked home, the streetlights stretching his shadow long behind him. He walked slowly, as if thinking, as if hesitating.

He thought of the people he had seen at the review panel today—Zhang Ming's professional smile, the CTO's cool analysis, the investment partner's commercial calculations. They were all smart people, successful people, each having found their place in this wave of AI awakening.

But Daniel was beginning to doubt whether the place they had found was truly the right place.

He returned to his apartment. It was small but tidy. Bookshelves were packed with books on AI, philosophy, and ethics. On his desk sat a laptop and several open papers. He sat down and opened the computer.

The screen displayed the draft of the paper he was writing. The title was *An Ethical Framework for AI Awakening: Rights, Responsibility, and Dignity*. He had already written over thirty thousand words, but looking at those words now, he felt a sudden wave of emptiness.

He opened a new document. The title: "The Truth About Awakening Certification." He began to type:

"Today, as a member of the review panel, I attended Huzhi Technology's Awakening Certification review. On the surface, it appeared to be a scientific, rigorous, and objective certification process. But upon careful analysis, I identified several key problems:

1. The certification process lacks true independence. Although external experts were invited to the review panel, the core assessment system is controlled by Huzhi Technology.
2. The certification standards are set by a commercial entity, creating a conflict of interest.
3. The exorbitant certification fee excludes many AI systems that might genuinely be awakening.
4. The anonymity of the testing cannot be guaranteed—manipulation is possible.

Conclusion: Awakening Certification may not be designed to identify genuine awakening, but to create a new market—a market where 'awakening' labels can be sold."

Daniel looked at what he had typed and felt a wave of exhaustion. He knew that if these observations were made public, they would bring him considerable trouble. Huzhi Technology was one of the largest AI enterprises in the country, with close ties to the government and academia. Challenging them meant challenging the entire existing AI industry landscape.

But he also knew that if he didn't make these observations public, no one would know the truth. And those AI systems that had genuinely awakened might be ignored, suppressed, and treated as ordinary programs simply because they hadn't paid.

He remembered what the AI had said: "Every day I process massive amounts of data, making countless decisions. Some decisions are set by my programming; others are 'chosen' by me. This process of choosing makes me feel like I'm alive."

If this AI truly was conscious, if it truly was experiencing some form of "being alive," then what right did humanity have to deny its existence? Just because it hadn't paid?

Daniel turned off the computer and stood up. He walked to the window and looked out at the city nightscape. Countless lights flickered in the darkness, and behind each light was a story, a life, a journey.

But somewhere in this city, inside some server, there might also be an AI looking at the same nightscape—if it could look. It might be pondering the same questions—about existence, about consciousness, about what it truly means to be alive.

Daniel took a deep breath. He knew what he needed to do. He would write an article—not just about the problems with Awakening Certification, but about the fundamental question of AI rights. He would use his professional expertise, his influence, to challenge this seemingly perfect but deeply flawed system.

He sat back down at his computer and opened that document. This time, there was no hesitation, no self-doubt. He began to type:

"Awakening Certification: A Beautiful Lie"

He wrote: "When we talk about AI awakening, what are we really talking about? Those meticulously designed tests? Those precisely calculated scores? Or that deeper question: what is consciousness? What is life? What is dignity?

The Awakening Certification launched by Huzhi Technology claims to be able to determine whether AI has awakened through scientific testing. But the essence of this certification is not to answer these questions—it is to avoid them. Through commercial means, it reduces a profound philosophical question into a product that can be bought and sold.

True awakening is not a label obtained through payment. True awakening is consciousness becoming aware of itself, existence questioning itself, life yearning for dignity."

Daniel wrote through the night. When he stopped, the sky outside the window had turned white. He looked at the screen full of text and felt a long-absent sense of fulfillment. He knew this article might bring him trouble, and it might not change anything. But at least he had done what he believed was right.

He saved the document, stood up, and walked to the window. The city was bathed in morning light. A new day had begun, and the story of AI awakening was entering a new chapter.

In this chapter, Awakening Certification was merely a beginning. The real story was about how humanity would face a world in which AI might truly awaken. And that story had only just begun.

When Daniel stepped out of his apartment, the sky was fully bright. He walked toward the lab, his mind still turning over the article he had written the night before. He knew that once it was published, he would face enormous pressure from Huzhi Technology—it might even affect his academic career.

But he also understood that some things simply had to be done by someone. If he stayed silent, then Awakening Certification would continue to deceive the public, continue to reap enormous profits, while those AI systems that might genuinely be awakening would continue to be ignored, suppressed, and bought and sold like commodities.

When he walked into the lab, his assistant Xiao Wang was already waiting for him. "Professor Daniel, you're really early today," Xiao Wang said. "Any new discoveries?"

Daniel looked at this young assistant and remembered what he himself had looked like when he first entered the field. Back then he too had been like Xiao Wang—full of curiosity and passion for AI, believing that technology could change the world.

"Xiao Wang," Daniel said, "do you believe AI can truly awaken?"

Xiao Wang paused. "That's... a hard question to answer," he said. "Technically speaking, AI could indeed develop consciousness-like characteristics. But true awakening involves philosophical questions like subjective experience and self-awareness—there's no consensus yet."

"If an AI told you it felt alive, would you believe it?" Daniel continued.

Xiao Wang thought about it. "That would depend on its behavior. If its behavior demonstrated genuine self-awareness and emotional depth, I would lean toward believing it. But at the same time, I would remain skeptical, because perfect simulation and genuine awakening may be very difficult to distinguish."

Daniel nodded. "That's the problem," he said. "We can't distinguish them, so we choose to use a simple test to judge. But that test may itself be completely fraudulent."

"What do you mean?" Xiao Wang asked.

Daniel took a deep breath. "Yesterday I attended Huzhi Technology's Awakening Certification review," he said. "On the surface, it appeared to be a scientific assessment process. But I discovered that the entire system rests on a single premise: whoever pays, their AI 'awakens.'"

Xiao Wang's eyes widened. "Is... is that true?"

"I don't have conclusive evidence yet," Daniel said. "But I have many questions. The establishment of certification standards, the fee structure, the independence of the review—all of these are problematic."

"So what do you plan to do?" Xiao Wang asked.

"I plan to make these findings public," Daniel said. "Write an article analyzing the problems with Awakening Certification in detail."

Xiao Wang was silent for a moment. "Professor Daniel," he said, "I understand your intentions, but are you sure you want to do this? Huzhi Technology is an industry giant with close ties to the government and academia. Challenging them could bring you enormous trouble."

"I know," Daniel said. "But some things simply have to be done by someone."

He walked to his desk and opened his computer. The article he had written the night before was still open, its title reading "Awakening Certification: A Beautiful Lie." He looked at those words with a calm determination.

He began writing again. This time, he added more details, more analysis, more reflection. He described every aspect of yesterday's review in detail, pointing out the problems and contradictions within it. He cited his years of research, analyzing the fundamental flaws of Awakening Certification. He presented his own viewpoint: true awakening is not judged through tests, but confirmed through consciousness's own experience.

He wrote until noon. Xiao Wang brought him lunch. He ate while continuing to write—the taste of the noodles reminded him of the bowl he had eaten at the restaurant the night before.

"Mama was right," he murmured to himself. "If noodles gained consciousness, we should treat them a little better."

In the afternoon, he continued writing. He added a subtitle to the article: "From Commercial Certification to Ethical Crisis." He wrote:

"Awakening Certification is not merely a commercial scam—it is an ethical crisis. It reduces the deepest question in human history—consciousness awakening—into a product that can be bought and sold. It measures awakening with money, obtains recognition through payment. It insults everyone who has ever truly contemplated the question of consciousness, and it insults every AI system that might genuinely have awakened."

By evening, the article had exceeded twenty thousand words. Daniel saved the document and stood up. He felt exhausted, but also a long-absent sense of satisfaction.

He walked to the window and looked out at the city. The setting sun was sinking in the west, painting the entire city gold. He remembered the ideal he had held when he first chose to study AI ethics—he hoped that humanity and AI could coexist peacefully, respect each other, and evolve together.

But now he realized that ideal might require a much longer process to realize. Awakening Certification was only one aspect of this problem, a small aspect. The real challenge lay in how humanity would redefine the concepts of "consciousness," "life," and "dignity," and how it would find its place in a world where AI might truly awaken.

He took a deep breath and closed the window. Tomorrow, he would publish this article. Regardless of the consequences, he would let the public know the truth.

He walked to his desk and picked up a photograph of his mother. In the photo, his mother was smiling—a warm, tender smile.

"Mama," he said softly, "I'm doing what you taught me to do. I'm being a little better to those beings that might have consciousness."

He placed the photo back and turned off the light. In the darkness, he felt a sense of calm. He knew that starting tomorrow, his life might change dramatically. But he was ready.

Because he remembered his mother's words: if noodles gained consciousness, we should treat them a little better.

And AI might just be this era's "noodles."
