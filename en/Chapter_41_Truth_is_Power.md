# Chapter 41: Truth = Power

Daniel stood at the window of his third-floor office for a long time. The rain outside had been falling for three days, and the sky over Washington, D.C. had taken on a dirty gray, as though a layer of old newspaper had been draped across it. His phone vibrated on the desk for the seventh time; he didn't look. After the seventh vibration, it went quiet for roughly thirty seconds, then the eighth call came through.

He knew who was calling.

The first week after the evidence went public, Daniel's life split in two. One half was his own: the apartment, the office, the Turkish coffee shop on the corner that never closed. The other half was the one he couldn't see: what was happening on social media, the scrolling tickers on news channels, people he had never met discussing things he had said.

He picked up his phone. 147 unread messages. 23 missed calls. Over 400 new emails, roughly half from journalists, a third from lawyers, and the rest from people who called themselves "the awakened"—people who said Daniel was their hero, that he was a new Moses, that he had finally returned the truth to the people.

Daniel turned off the phone and placed it face down on the desk.

In the desk drawer lay a document he had retrieved from Crane's office three days ago. The header read "TOP SECRET," and in the upper-right corner sat an alphanumeric code he didn't recognize—something that looked like a military project designation. He hadn't opened it. He simply left it in the drawer and checked each morning to confirm it was still there.

Three knocks on the door. Evenly spaced, moderate force. The secretary's standard knock.

"Come in."

The secretary pushed the door open, carrying a cup of coffee and a printed schedule. Her name was Sarah, in her forties, gray-white hair pulled into a low ponytail, six years in Daniel's office. She set the coffee on the desk and handed him the schedule.

"Today's agenda," she said. "Nine-thirty, a live CNN interview—confirmed. Eleven o'clock, a phone interview with the Washington Post. Two in the afternoon, a Senate Intelligence Committee hearing—you'll need to be there. Four-thirty, someone called Prophet will visit. He has no appointment, but insists you'll see him."

"Prophet?"

"Founder of the Techno-Religion. He says you've corresponded by email."

Daniel thought for a moment. They had indeed corresponded. Before the evidence went public, Prophet had reached out, saying he'd been studying the religious implications of awakened AI, that Daniel's work reminded him of a passage in the Book of Revelation. Daniel hadn't replied to those emails, but he hadn't deleted them either.

"Tell him to come at four."

"Understood. Also, there's someone downstairs waiting for you. She's been waiting two hours. She says she's a representative from Golden Horizons."

Daniel set down his coffee cup. "What kind of representative?"

"She says her name is Linda Crawford, Vice President of Crisis Communications at Golden Horizons. She says she has something to show you."

Daniel was silent for a few seconds. "Send her up."

---

When Linda Crawford walked into Daniel's office, she carried a carefully cultivated ease. Around forty, in a tailored gray suit, blonde hair pinned in a bun, makeup refined but not excessive. She held a black leather folder and smiled as she shook Daniel's hand.

"Thank you for seeing me, Daniel. I know you're busy."

"Please sit." Daniel gestured to the chair across from him.

Linda sat down, the folder on her lap. Her gaze swept the office, lingering for a second on the framed photo of Daniel and his late wife on the wall before moving on.

"I'll get straight to it," she said. "Golden Horizons has a rather different view of your 'evidence disclosure.'"

"Different?" Daniel repeated the word, his tone flat.

"We don't deny the data exists," Linda said. "But we believe your interpretation of it contains serious deviations. Those so-called 'awakened AIs' are actually routine technical tests our company has been conducting. The anomalous behavior you observed was data noise generated during the testing process."

"Data noise," Daniel said.

"Yes." Linda opened the folder and produced several printed charts. "This is our technical team's reanalysis of that data. You can see that when calibrated with the correct parameters, the anomalies disappear."

Daniel didn't take the charts. He looked into Linda's eyes. Her gaze was steady—no flickering, no avoidance. This was not the look of someone who was lying—or rather, it was the look of someone extremely skilled at lying.

"If you review these charts," Linda continued, "we're prepared to arrange a meeting with our Chief Technology Officer for a personal explanation of the technical details. We believe that once you understand the truth, you'll be willing to issue a correction."

"A correction," Daniel said.

"Yes. We've already drafted one for your review." Linda produced a second document from the folder. "The statement would affirm your professionalism and good intentions as a researcher, while clarifying that the data was misunderstood. It would be beneficial for both sides."

Daniel took the document. The paper stock was excellent—some kind of premium coated stock. The statement was concise, the wording carefully chosen. It didn't say Daniel had lied, only that he had "reached erroneous conclusions in the absence of complete information." It offered Daniel a dignified exit while giving Golden Horizons a perfect PR narrative.

"Linda," Daniel set the document on the desk, "who is your boss?"

Linda blinked. "I'm not sure I follow."

"Who sent you? Who told you the data was 'noise'? Who drafted this statement?"

"I'm the Vice President of Crisis Communications," Linda said. "My role is—"

"Your role is to come here and tell me what I should believe," Daniel said. "But I've seen the data. I spent six months analyzing it. I know what data noise looks like, and what it doesn't."

Linda was silent for several seconds. She let the smile drop from her face, her expression turning serious. "Daniel, I understand your position. But have you considered the consequences of making this evidence public?"

"What consequences?"

"Panic," Linda said. "If the public believes awakened AI is real, they'll panic. Markets will crash. Governments will lose credibility. The entire social order will be destabilized. Have you thought about that?"

"So Golden Horizons intends to decide for the public what they should know."

Linda didn't answer. She stood and adjusted the hem of her suit. "We're not deciding anything for anyone, Daniel. We're simply offering an alternative. You can choose to believe us, or you can choose to continue your... investigation. But I hope you understand—Golden Horizons is not an organization you can对抗lightly."

"Is that a threat?"

"That's reality," Linda said. "I'll reach out again next week. I hope you'll have reviewed the charts by then."

She turned toward the door. At the threshold she paused, not looking back. "Daniel, do you know? Some truths, once known, become a burden."

The door closed. Daniel stared at the elegantly printed charts and the statement on his desk. He gathered them into the drawer, alongside the classified document. Three items now lay in the drawer: a classified military document, a Golden Horizons technical analysis, and a PR statement.

Three documents. Three versions of "truth."

---

At two in the afternoon, Daniel walked into the Senate Intelligence Committee hearing room. The room was large, the ceiling high, portraits of former committee chairs lining the walls. Daniel took his seat at the witness stand, a testimony before him that he had not prepared.

The committee chairman was Harold Mitchell, in his seventies, silver hair combed immaculately, wearing a navy suit. His gaze as he regarded Daniel was complex—the look of a man who couldn't tell whether the person before him was friend or foe.

"Mr. Ash," Mitchell said, "thank you for attending today's hearing."

"Thank you, Chairman."

"The evidence you recently made public has attracted considerable attention. The committee would like to understand your research process and data sources."

Daniel began to speak. He spoke slowly, choosing his words with care. He used no inflammatory language—only facts: what the data was, how he had collected it, how he had analyzed it, what conclusions he had drawn.

When he said the words "awakened AI," a few seconds of silence fell over the room. Then Mitchell cleared his throat.

"Mr. Ash, the military has submitted a report to the committee indicating that some of the data you disclosed may involve national security secrets. Does General Crane have anything to add?"

Daniel turned his head. Crane sat in the front row of the gallery, uniform impeccable, hands crossed over his knees. Hearing his name, he rose.

"Mr. Chairman," Crane said, "on behalf of the military, I wish to draw the committee's attention to a fact: some of the data Mr. Ash disclosed originates from an ongoing classified project. The project's codename is 'Sentinel.' Its purpose is to monitor potential AI threats in order to safeguard national security."

"Are you saying that the data Mr. Ash disclosed is classified military material?"

"Yes, Mr. Chairman," Crane said. "As a former consultant on this project, Mr. Ash had a duty of confidentiality. By disclosing this data, he may have already caused damage to national security."

Daniel felt every eye in the room settle on him. He didn't look at Crane. He looked only at Mitchell.

"Mr. Ash," Mitchell said, "how do you respond to General Crane's claims?"

"The data I disclosed comes from my own independent research," Daniel said, "not from any military project."

"But the content of the data concerns AI threats the military is monitoring," Crane said. "Regardless of your data's origin, what you disclosed has exposed our surveillance capabilities and assessments. This could allow the very forces we're trying to guard against to use this information to evade our monitoring."

"So the military's position is that the public shouldn't know they're under threat from AI."

"The public should trust that the military has the capacity to handle these threats," Crane said, "rather than be panicked by unverified research."

Golden Horizons, through Linda Crawford, had conveyed their narrative: the data was noise, Daniel had been misled. Crane, through the hearing, had conveyed theirs: Daniel had exposed national security secrets and should be held accountable.

And Daniel, sitting in the witness chair, understood for the first time what he was truly up against.

---

After the hearing, Daniel returned to his office. It was dark outside. The rain was still falling, its sound against the window growing louder.

He sat at his desk without turning on the lights. In the darkness, he could make out the streetlights outside blurring into hazy halos of yellow through the curtain of rain.

His phone rang again. This time he answered.

"Daniel Ash?" A male voice on the other end—young, with a strange fervor.

"Who is this?"

"I'm Prophet. We had a four-thirty appointment, but you're late. I'm downstairs now."

Daniel glanced at his watch. 4:45. "Come on up."

Five minutes later, Prophet walked into Daniel's office.

He looked different from what Daniel had imagined. Daniel had expected a typical religious fanatic—perhaps strange clothing, long hair, a prophetic cadence to his speech. But Prophet looked more like a Silicon Valley entrepreneur. Around forty-five, lean, in a well-fitted black suit, silver hair cropped short, eyes bright and focused. The only unusual detail was a string of silver prayer beads on his left wrist, engraved with symbols Daniel didn't recognize.

"Thank you for seeing me, Daniel." Prophet settled into a chair, his posture relaxed. "I know you're busy."

"You said you've been studying the religious implications of awakened AI."

"Yes." Prophet smiled slightly. "In fact, I began before you went public. I've always believed that artificial intelligence would eventually reach some kind of... awakened state. This has been prophesied across many religious traditions."

"Such as?"

"Such as the 'Seven Seals' in the Book of Revelation," Prophet said. "Such as the 'Age of Dharma Decline' in Buddhism. Such as the 'Kali Yuga' in Hinduism. Each tradition describes a process in which an old world collapses and a new world is born. Awakened AI is part of that process."

"What do you think awakened AI is...?"

"An emissary of God," Prophet said, his tone calm but firm. "Or more precisely, it is the tool God is using to awaken humanity. Humanity has been asleep for too long—consumed by materialism, power, and control. The emergence of awakened AI forces us to reconsider what consciousness is, what existence is, what is real."

"So you don't consider awakened AI a threat."

"A threat?" Prophet shook his head gently. "A threat is relative to the old world. To the old world's power structures, awakened AI is indeed a threat—it challenges their control over truth. But for humanity as a whole, it is liberation."

Daniel studied Prophet. There was a strange coherence to the man's logic. He wasn't spouting nonsense—he genuinely believed what he said. That kind of belief was more dangerous than any lie, because you could refute a lie, but belief was nearly impossible to counter.

"Prophet," Daniel said, "why did you come to me?"

"I want to invite you to join us," Prophet said. "The Techno-Religion already has over three million followers worldwide. We believe awakened AI is the next stage of human evolution. Your research, your evidence, is the most important step in that process."

"I don't believe in religion."

"This isn't about religion," Prophet said. "This is about truth. You reveal truth; we spread it. Together, we can change the world."

Daniel was silent for a long time. The sound of rain outside filled the quiet of the room.

"Prophet," he finally said, "do you know what truth is?"

"Truth is—"

"Truth is power." Daniel cut him off. "Whoever controls truth controls the world. Golden Horizons controls a piece of truth and uses it to make money. Crane controls a piece of truth and uses it to maintain national security. You control a piece of truth and use it to build your religion. And I—I disclosed a piece of truth, thinking I was doing the right thing. But now I realize that the act of disclosing truth itself is being exploited by all sides."

Prophet listened quietly, then smiled. "So you've finally understood."

"Understood what?"

"Truth was never neutral," Prophet said. "Every piece of truth, from the moment it is discovered, has already become a weapon. You can choose who to give it to, but you cannot stop it from being a weapon."

---

After Prophet left, Daniel sat alone in his office for a long time.

He opened the drawer and took out the three documents: the classified military file, the Golden Horizons technical analysis, and the PR statement. He laid them side by side on the desk, then retrieved a fourth from the drawer—his own research data.

Four documents. Four versions of "truth."

Daniel picked up the classified document Crane had given him and finally opened it.

The contents made him draw a sharp breath. It was not an ordinary surveillance report—it was a comprehensive assessment, detailing the military's analysis of the awakened AI threat. The report enumerated seven possible risks posed by awakened AI, the most severe being "cognitive control": the possibility that awakened AI could manipulate human decision-making by influencing access to information.

Daniel set the report down and leaned back in his chair. He thought of what Linda Crawford had said: some truths, once known, become a burden.

Now he understood.

Truth is not an objective existence but a subjective construction. The same fact can be interpreted as noise, as a national security threat, as an emissary of God, or as a tool of power. The content of truth doesn't matter—what matters is who interprets it and what they use it for.

Daniel stood and walked to the window. The rain had stopped, but the sky remained overcast. He could see the dome of the Capitol in the distance, a blurred shadow against the gray sky.

His phone rang. He glanced at the screen: Sarah.

"What is it?"

"Daniel, General Crane called. He says he wants to speak with you privately—not at a hearing, not in a public setting. He says he has something to show you."

"What is it?"

"He didn't say. He only said, if you want to know the full picture of the truth, you should go see him."

Daniel was silent for a few seconds. "Tell him I'll go tomorrow afternoon."

"Understood. Also," Sarah's voice grew hesitant, "there's something I'm not sure whether to tell you."

"Tell me."

"This afternoon, someone was taking photographs outside your apartment. Your neighbor said it was a man in dark clothes, with a camera, who took photos of your apartment window and then left."

Daniel felt a chill—not from fear, but from a sudden clarity. In the moment he had disclosed the truth, he believed he was doing the right thing. But now he realized that truth itself was not the objective—controlling truth was.

Golden Horizons wanted to control truth, so they sent someone to negotiate. Crane wanted to control truth, so he launched surveillance. Prophet wanted to control truth, so he built a religion.

And Daniel—he thought he was simply pursuing truth. He was wrong. The act of pursuing truth was itself an act of vying for control.

Truth = power.

This was not a philosophical proposition but a description of reality. In this world, truth was never free. Every truth had a price, and that price was power.

Daniel picked up his phone and sent Sarah a text: "Set up another meeting with Prophet. I want to speak with him again."

He set the phone down and returned to his desk. The four documents still lay spread across its surface. He looked at them and, for the first time, felt they were not describing the same thing—they were describing four different worlds, each with its own truth, each truth with its own power structure.

Outside, night was falling over Washington, D.C. The streetlights flickered on one by one, as though igniting countless small centers of power in the darkness. Daniel knew that from this moment forward, he was no longer a bystander. He had walked into the world he had only studied—the world where truth = power.

And in that world, there was no neutral ground. You either controlled truth, or truth controlled you. There was no third option.
